Key Categorical Observations:
  * Checking Account Status: Borrowers with negative/no balance (A11: <0 DM) have an alarming
    default rate of ~49.3%, whereas borrowers with >=200 DM (A13) or no checking account (A14)
    default at only 22.2% and 11.7% respectively. This is a primary risk discriminator.
  * Credit History: Borrowers with critical accounts / active credits elsewhere (A34) show surprisingly
    lower default rates (17.1%) than borrowers with delayed payments or all loans paid duly,
    reflecting established credit bureau activity and seasoned borrowing history.
  * Savings Status: Higher liquid savings (A64: >=1000 DM) drastically correlates with repayment
    (default rate 12.5% vs. ~36.0% for <100 DM savings).

5. DATASET LIMITATIONS & EXPLAINABILITY (XAI) IMPLICATIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Modest Sample Size: 1,000 instances is ideal for fast prototyping and XAI visual verification,
    but demands cross-validation to prevent tree-ensemble overfitting.
  * Sensitive Features (Fair Lending): Attributes such as personal_status (encodes gender/marital)
    and foreign_worker represent protected demographic features. Under regulations such as ECOA
    and GDPR, XAI (SHAP/LIME) will play a crucial role in auditing whether these features disproportionately
    bias loan approval decisions.
  * Discretization: Certain continuous financial quantities were originally discretized into bins
    (e.g., checking_status A11..A14, savings_status A61..A65), which simplifies local surrogate explanations.
